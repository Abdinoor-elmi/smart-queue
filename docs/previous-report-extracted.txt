ABSTRACT
The Smart Queue Management System (SQMS) is an innovative web-based platform engi-
neered to modernize queue management operations at customer-facing service organizations.
By automating critical processes such as ticket issuance, counter assignment, real-time queue
status broadcasting, and service time analytics, SQMS addresses the inefficiencies of traditional
manual queue management systems.
Developed using the Agile methodology, the system incorporates role-based access control for
administrators, staff, kiosk users, and public display viewers, alongside WebSocket-powered
real-time updates to ensure all stakeholders have instant visibility into queue state.
SQMS aligns with contemporary digital transformation trends in service delivery, delivering
a scalable, lightweight, and efficient solution that elevates operational efficiency and fosters a
seamless service experience for customers and staff alike.

1 INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background
Traditional queue management systems are reliant on manual processes and often struggle with
inefficiencies in ticket issuance, counter assignment, and real-time status visibility for both
customers and staff. Long wait times, disorganized service areas, and the absence of data-driven
insights into service performance limit the effectiveness of service organizations globally. The
rise of digital technologies presents a significant opportunity to transform these operations into
streamlined, transparent, and user-centric services.
The Smart Queue Management System (SQMS) leverages modern web technologies, includ-
ing Node.js, Express.js, SQLite, and the WebSocket protocol, to deliver a sophisticated yet
lightweight solution for service organizations. By introducing automation and real-time com-
munication, SQMS aims to enhance operational efficiency, improve customer experience, and
elevate overall service quality for customers, staff, and administrators.
1.2 Problem Statement
Service organizations that rely on traditional queue management face significant challenges,
including:
1. Manual ticket issuance requiring dedicated reception staff, increasing operational costs.
2. Inability for staff to efficiently track and prioritize queue positions across multiple counters.
3. Absence of historical service data for analyzing and improving average service times.
4. High cost or infrastructure complexity of commercial queue management solutions.
These limitations result in poor customer satisfaction, staff inefficiency, and missed opportuni-
ties for continuous service improvement. SQMS addresses these issues through a self-service
kiosk, a real-time display board, and a data-driven wait time estimation engine.
1.3 Objectives
The SQMS project aims:
1. To develop a web-based queue management system with self-service ticket issuance via a
kiosk interface.
2. To implement real-time queue status broadcasting to all clients using the WebSocket proto-
col.
8

3. To automate the full ticket lifecycle from issuance through calling, serving, and completion.
4. To implement secure session-based authentication with role-based access control for admin-
istrators, staff, and display viewers.
5. To provide service time analytics through a moving average calculation on completed ser-
vice transactions.
1.4 Scope
The SQMS focuses on automating core queue management functions through a responsive web-
based platform accessible on desktops, laptops, tablets, and display screens via standard web
browsers. The system provides four distinct interfaces: a customer self-service kiosk, a staff
queue management dashboard, an administrator configuration panel, and a public display board
for real-time service status.
The system supports multiple service types and multiple service counters, with each counter
assigned to a specific service. This phase excludes the development of a native mobile appli-
cation, SMS or email notifications, integration with third-party hardware ticket printers, and
offline operation, which may be explored in future iterations.
1.5 Significance
SQMS delivers substantial benefits across all stakeholder groups. Customers benefit from self-
service ticket issuance and accurate real-time wait time estimates, reducing perceived waiting
time and frustration. Staff benefit from a centralized queue dashboard that eliminates the need
to manage physical queues manually, enabling them to focus fully on service delivery. Admin-
istrators gain visibility into service performance and can configure services, counters, and user
accounts without technical intervention.
By aligning with digital transformation trends in public and private sector service organizations,
SQMS improves operational efficiency and fosters a transparent, data-driven service environ-
ment that supports continuous improvement.
1.6 Justification
The project addresses a genuine operational pain point common to government service offices,
banks, hospitals, and retail organizations. Commercial queue management solutions such as Q-
Nomy and Qmatic require substantial licensing investment and complex proprietary infrastruc-
ture that is inaccessible to smaller organizations. SQMS provides a fully open-source, browser-
based alternative that can be deployed on commodity cloud infrastructure, removing hardware
9

dependencies and democratizing access to professional queue management capabilities for or-
ganizations of all sizes.

2 REVIEW OF SIMILAR SYSTEMS
2.1 Overview of Similar Systems
Several queue management solutions exist, but most are expensive enterprise products that re-
quire proprietary hardware, vendor-specific infrastructure, or ongoing subscription fees. Below
are three existing systems and how they compare to the proposed Smart Queue Management
System.
2.1.1 Q-Nomy
Q-Nomy is an enterprise queue management and customer journey platform offering compre-
hensive features, including appointment scheduling, virtual queuing, multi-branch support, and
advanced analytics dashboards. It is widely used by large-scale organizations such as banks,
telecommunications companies, and government agencies.
While Q-Nomy provides highly sophisticated functionality, it requires significant financial in-
vestment in licensing and often demands dedicated on-premise hardware for kiosk terminals
and display systems. Its complexity and cost make it unsuitable for smaller organizations or
those seeking a lightweight, browser-based deployment. The proposed SQMS delivers core
queue management capabilities in a fully open-source, browser-based solution that requires no
proprietary hardware.
2.1.2 Qmatic
Qmatic is a Swedish enterprise customer journey management system widely used in health-
care, banking, and government sectors. It provides physical queue management hardware, cus-
tomer feedback collection, multi-language support, and business intelligence reporting inte-
grated into a comprehensive platform.
Qmatic excels in enterprise-scale deployments requiring dedicated hardware integration, but its
high total cost of ownership and infrastructure requirements make it inaccessible to medium and
small organizations. The SQMS addresses this gap by delivering real-time queue management
entirely through standard web browsers and an embedded SQLite database, requiring only a
server capable of running Node.js.
2.1.3 Skiplino
Skiplino is a cloud-based smart queue management system that allows customers to book their
place in queues remotely via a mobile application. It targets retail, healthcare, and government
sectors and provides real-time analytics, customer feedback tools, and multi-branch manage-
ment through a subscription-based model.
11

Skiplino offers greater customer convenience through its mobile application, but it depends
entirely on its proprietary cloud infrastructure and requires ongoing subscription fees. The
proposed SQMS offers a self-hostable, open-source alternative that organizations can deploy
on their own infrastructure, maintain full control of their data, and operate without recurring
licensing costs.
2.2 Gaps in Current Systems
Analysis of existing systems reveals several critical gaps:
1. The high cost and complexity of enterprise solutions make them inaccessible to smaller
service organizations and public sector institutions with limited budgets.
2. Dependence on proprietary hardware for kiosk terminals and display boards creates vendor
lock-in and increases the total cost of ownership.
3. No lightweight, open-source solution combines real-time WebSocket broadcasting, role-
based access control, and moving-average service time analytics in a single deployable
package.
2.3 The Proposed System
The SQMS distinguishes itself by integrating self-service ticket issuance, real-time WebSocket
broadcasting, role-based access control, and moving-average service time analytics into a co-
hesive, lightweight platform built on Node.js and SQLite. Unlike existing enterprise solutions,
SQMS is fully open-source, requires no proprietary hardware, and can be deployed on com-
modity cloud infrastructure, making it an accessible and transformative solution for service
organizations of all sizes.

3 SYSTEM ANALYSIS AND DESIGN
3.1 Systems Analysis and Design Methodology
The SQMS project adopts the Agile Software Development Methodology, valued for its iterative
and adaptive approach. Agile facilitates continuous improvement through incremental develop-
ment cycles, enabling the system to evolve based on user feedback and emerging requirements.
This methodology supports rapid prototyping, frequent testing, and user-centric design, mak-
ing it an ideal framework for a queue management system whose requirements were refined
iteratively through observation of real service environments.

3.2 System Analysis
3.2.1 Requirements Elicitation
This process employed a combination of methods, including interviews, observations of existing
queue environments, and use case scenarios to ensure a comprehensive understanding of both
functional and non-functional requirements.
Interviews:Interviews with primary stakeholders, including service counter staff, supervisors,
and customers, were conducted to identify pain points in existing queue systems and gather
insights into desired features for a digital queue management platform. These discussions fo-
cused on the need for efficient ticket tracking, real-time visibility, and automation of queue
assignment.
Observations:Direct observation of service environments, including bank branches and gov-
ernment service counters, provided practical insight into queue flow patterns, peak load periods,
and the specific interactions between customers and service staff that the system needed to sup-
port.
Use Cases:Use cases were created to outline how various user roles, including customers at
the kiosk, service staff, administrators, and display viewers, interact with the system. These
scenarios defined functional requirements related to ticket issuance, counter assignment, queue
status broadcasting, and system administration.
Developing the Smart Queue Management System leads to the following improvements:
1.Streamlined Operations:Transitioning to an automated system replaces manual ticket
issuance, reducing errors in queue tracking and improving the speed of service transactions.
2.Enhanced Visibility:The real-time display board provides customers with immediate vis-
ibility into service status, reducing uncertainty and perceived waiting time.
3.Time Efficiency:Automation of queue ordering and counter assignment reduces the ad-
ministrative burden on staff, allowing them to focus entirely on serving customers.
3.2.2 Requirements Analysis
Based on the outlined requirements, the proposed system has the following functionalities:
Functional Requirements
1.Self-Service Ticket Issuance:Customers select their desired service from the kiosk and
receive a uniquely numbered ticket together with an estimated wait time based on the current
14

queue length and historical service averages.
2.Real-Time Queue Broadcasting:All connected clients receive instant updates via Web-
Socket whenever a ticket is issued, called, or completed, ensuring consistent queue state
across the kiosk, staff dashboard, and display board.
3.Staff Queue Management:Authenticated staff can view the current queue for their as-
signed service, call the next ticket, mark it as serving, and complete service, advancing the
ticket through its full lifecycle.
4.Counter Assignment:Each service counter is configured to handle a specific service type.
When a ticket is called, it is associated with the calling counter so the display board can
direct customers accurately.
5.Admin System Configuration:Administrators can create and manage services, counters,
and user accounts through a dedicated admin panel, with full CRUD operations supported
via a REST API.
6.Service Time Analytics:The system calculates estimated wait times using a moving aver-
age of the last 20 completed service transactions per service type, providing dynamic and
accurate predictions.
7.Public Display Board:A dedicated view shows customers which ticket numbers are cur-
rently being served and at which counter, providing real-time service status without requir-
ing authentication.
Non-Functional Requirements
1.Performance:The system must handle concurrent WebSocket connections from all active
kiosk terminals, staff workstations, and display boards simultaneously without degradation.
2.Security:All user authentication must use bcrypt password hashing, and access to staff and
admin functions must be protected by session-based authentication with role verification on
every API request.
3.Scalability:The system must support future expansion to additional service types and coun-
ters, with the SQLite database replaceable by a more scalable RDBMS as load increases.
4.Usability:Each interface must be purpose-designed for its specific context, ensuring a
seamless experience for users of varying technical proficiency.
5.Compatibility:The system must be accessible on major web browsers on desktops, laptops,
tablets, and display hardware.
15

6.Maintainability:The system is structured with clear separation between API routes, database
logic, and frontend assets, facilitating easy updates and debugging.
7.Data Integrity:SQLite foreign key constraints are enforced across all related tables to
preserve the accuracy of ticket, counter, and transaction records.

3.3 System Design
3.3.1 Architectural Design
SQMS follows a single-server client-server architecture. The Express.js server simultaneously
serves static HTML/CSS/JavaScript assets to browser clients and exposes a REST API for data
operations. A WebSocket server runs on the same port, enabling bidirectional real-time com-
munication. All persistent data is stored in an embedded SQLite database, minimizing infras-
tructure requirements while delivering real-time capabilities.

3.3.3 Database Design
The SQMS database consists of six relational tables:roles,users,services,counters,
tickets, andservice_transactions. Foreign key constraints are defined across all related
tables to ensure referential integrity. Theservice_transactionstable records the start and
completion time of each service interaction, forming the basis for the moving average wait time
calculation.

4 SYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION
4.1 Resources
4.1.1 Hardware
Development and testing were conducted on a laptop with 8GB RAM and an Intel Core i5
processor, providing sufficient performance for coding, running the local Node.js development
server, and validating WebSocket real-time behaviour across multiple simultaneous browser
sessions, simulating different user roles.
4.1.2 Software
The development environment included Visual Studio Code as the primary code editor with
the ESLint extension for code quality, GitHub for version control, and the DB Browser for
SQLite desktop application for database inspection and schema validation during development.
Nodemon was used during development to enable automatic server restarts on file changes.
4.2 Technologies
SQMS was implemented using a modern, lightweight technology stack selected for minimal
infrastructure requirements and ease of deployment:
Frontend:Vanilla HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript with no framework dependency. Each of the
four views, kiosk, staff, admin, and display, has a dedicated HTML file with a corresponding
JavaScript module and stylesheet, keeping the bundle small and loading fast on any device.
Backend:Node.js with Express.js handles all server-side REST API routing, session manage-
ment, and static asset serving. The single-process architecture keeps deployment simple while
supporting all required concurrent connections.
Database:SQLite3 via thesqlite3npm package provides an embedded relational database
requiring no separate server process. The database path is configurable via an environment
variable to support both local development and cloud deployment.
Real-Time Communication:ThewsWebSocket library runs a WebSocket server on the same
HTTP server instance as Express. All queue state changes, ticket issuance, status updates, and
completions are broadcast in real time to all connected clients using a centralizedbroadcast()
function.
Authentication:bcryptis used for secure password hashing at account creation and login.
express-sessionmanages authenticated sessions with cookie security configured for pro-
32

duction deployment via environment variables.
Deployment:The application is containerized via a Dockerfile and deployed on Render.com
cloud infrastructure. Therender.yamlconfiguration file defines the web service, environment
variables, and build and start commands.

CONCLUSION
The Smart Queue Management System (SQMS) successfully modernizes queue management
operations by integrating self-service ticket issuance, real-time WebSocket broadcasting, role-
based access control, and moving-average service time analytics into a cohesive, open-source
web platform. Developed iteratively using the Agile methodology, SQMS eliminates manual
queue management inefficiencies, reduces customer wait time uncertainty, and empowers ser-
vice organizations with data-driven insights through dynamic wait time estimation.
Its fully browser-based design, embedded SQLite database, and containerized deployment elim-
inate proprietary hardware dependencies and make the system accessible to organizations of all
sizes at a fraction of the cost of commercial alternatives. The four purpose-built interfaces, cus-
tomer kiosk, staff dashboard, admin panel, and public display board, each serve a distinct role
in the service workflow, ensuring that all stakeholders have the right information at the right
time.
SQMS sets a practical standard for lightweight, modern queue management and provides a
strong foundation for future enhancements, including SMS and email notifications, mobile ap-
plication support, and multi-branch analytics.
